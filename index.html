Using the Tapo camera in your simulator room
A browser cannot open an RTSP stream directly

Tapo cameras stream over RTSP, and no browser on any platform can play RTSP. That is a protocol limitation, not something this app can code around. The stream has to be converted to something a browser understands first.

Enable RTSP on the camera

In the Tapo app, open the camera, tap the gear, then Advanced Settings and Camera Account. Create a username and password — this is separate from your TP-Link login. Your stream is then rtsp://user:pass@CAMERA-IP:554/stream1, or stream2 for the lighter one. Note that most battery and solar Tapo models do not offer RTSP at all; the mains-powered C1xx and C3xx cameras do.

Convert it to something Safari can play

You need one always-on device on your network running a bridge — go2rtc, MediaMTX and Scrypted all do this, and Home Assistant has it built in. Point the bridge at your RTSP URL and it gives you back an HLS address ending in .m3u8. Paste that into the field above and this app treats it exactly like a camera. A Raspberry Pi, a NAS, or any computer left running will do it; a phone or tablet will not.

The simpler option — a USB camera

iPadOS supports USB cameras over USB-C. Plug one in, tap Find cameras, and if it appears in the list you can position it down-the-line permanently and never touch the iPad camera again. No bridge, no network setup, and it is the cheapest path by some distance.

Without either, today

Record on the Tapo, export the clip from the Tapo app to your Photos, then import it in the Swing Lab. Slower, but it works with what you already own.

Setting up the bridge with your SkyTrak+
Why bother

Your chain is SkyTrak+ into the SkyTrak app, OpenSkyPlus2 reading it, GSPro4OSP pushing to GSPro on port 921. That hop carries angle of attack, club path, face angle, closure rate, dynamic loft and lie, spin axis and where on the face you struck it — none of which appears on screen. The bridge copies it to this app without touching anything.

Three steps, once

1. Put golf-lab-bridge.js and START-BRIDGE.bat in a folder on the PC and double-click the .bat.
2. In GSPro4OSP's pluginsettings.json, change "Port": 921 to "Port": 922. Leave Hostname alone. Restart SkyTrak.
3. Put ws://localhost:8080 above and press Start collecting.

Screen capture instead

Leave the bridge field empty and it captures the GSPro window, watching for the panel to change. Works with nothing installed, but only reads what is displayed.

Everything you need to run Golf Lab properly, downloadable from here. No internet required — these are built into the app itself.

Launch monitor bridge

These two go in a folder on the PC running GSPro. The bridge sits between GSPro4OSP and GSPro, passing every shot through untouched while handing Golf Lab a copy — so you get angle of attack, club path, face angle and everything else the screen never shows.

The bridge
golf-lab-bridge.js
No dependencies. Needs Node.js.
The launcher
START-BRIDGE.bat
Double-click to run. Checks for Node.js.
Setup — three steps, once
1 · Run the bridge

Put both files in the same folder on the PC and double-click START-BRIDGE.bat. If Node.js is missing it tells you where to get it — take the LTS option and accept the defaults.

2 · Point GSPro4OSP at it

Open pluginsettings.json in your SkyTrak BepInEx plugins folder and change "Port": 921 to "Port": 922. Leave Hostname as 127.0.0.1. Restart SkyTrak.

3 · Connect Golf Lab

On the Sessions tab set the source to Launch monitor — via bridge and the address to ws://localhost:8080.

GSPro itself needs no changes and never knows the bridge is there. Leave the black window open while you play.
